# Competitors: who helps Canadians find a whole trip for a budget

Researched 2026-11-25 from public pages and search results only (method: `founder-competitors`). Every fact links to its source. Where a source was thin, old or a third party, it says so. Prices and features change, so re-check before quoting any of this publicly.

**Short answer:** many tools answer "where can I fly for $X?". Package sellers answer "which all-inclusive costs $X?". Almost nobody answers the question our user actually has: **"I have $2,500 in total for two of us. Where can we go from Toronto, all costs in, and how sure is that number?"** That question is the gap. It is real but narrow, and Google is the one player that could close it fast (see "The threat").

**Substitutes** (how people do it today without a dedicated tool):
- A spreadsheet or notes app plus 5-10 browser tabs: Google Flights, then Booking.com or Airbnb, then a blog's "daily budget" post.
- Asking Reddit or a Facebook group "where can we go for $2,000?".
- Asking ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. These give fluent answers with no live prices and no stated confidence.
- Defaulting to an all-inclusive because the price is "all in", even when a city trip would suit them better.

## 2. Price range for the comparable item

There is no product that sells "a budget-first whole-trip search", so compare **what the user pays to get the answer**:

| Lowest | Typical | Highest |
|---|---|---|
| $0 (Google, Kayak, Skyscanner, free newsletters, our site) | $0, plus about 1-3 hours of tab-juggling | About $85-190 a year for paid deal clubs ([Triips](https://triips.com/faq); [Thrifty Traveler](https://thriftytraveler.com/Premium), U.S.-focused). Or Hopper add-ons, average Price Freeze about $30 (2022, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/07/hopper-launches-offerings-more-flexibility-before-during-trips/amp/)) |

The trips themselves cost the same everywhere, because the user books with the same suppliers. Sun packages from Toronto run about **$1,300-1,600 per person** for a week all-inclusive (Sunwing and RedTag examples above). That is a useful anchor: our results must make a city or non-resort trip look as easy to budget as an all-inclusive.

## 3. Positioning map (text)

Axes that matter to this buyer:
- **X:** how much of the trip the price covers (airfare only → the whole trip: flights, room, food and local costs).
- **Y:** where you start (pick a destination first → start from your budget).

```
                 START FROM BUDGET
                        ▲
 Google Flights Explore │                     ★ US (gap)
 Kayak Explore          │                    whole trip, budget-first,
 Skyscanner Everywhere  │                    honest ranges, CAD
 Deal newsletters       │
                        │        Google AI Mode (chat; budget is a prompt, not a model)
 AIRFARE ONLY ──────────┼───────────────────────────────────► WHOLE TRIP
                        │
 Hopper                 │   Expedia packages   RedTag / itravel2000 / Sunwing
                        │   (flight+hotel)     (all-inclusive, all-in price)

                        ▼
              START FROM DESTINATION
```

The top-right corner is empty except for Google AI Mode at the edge, and it treats budget as something you say, not something it models.

## 4. What their customers complain about

Recurring themes from public reviews and coverage. They are ranked by how much they matter to us, not by count, because the review sample is small. Themes with fewer than three sources are marked **thin**.

1. **"The price I saw isn't the price I pay": hidden fees, add-ons, drip pricing.**
   - Hopper settled with the FTC for **$35 million** over "optional" fees that were pre-selected and hidden, and over price protection with an undisclosed $300 cap ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/333933); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/travel-app-pay-35m-settle-ftc-allegations-it-charged-fees-without-consent)).
   - Kayak's map warns fares "may not include baggage fees" ([Kayak Canada](https://www.ca.kayak.com/news/where-to-fly-on-your-budget-kayak-explore)).
   - Expedia's "up to $4,200" savings line carries an asterisk ([Expedia.ca](https://www.expedia.ca/p/all-inclusive/toronto)).
   - **Use:** our whole pitch is all-in numbers with honest ranges. Never show a teaser price.
2. **Refunds and customer service when things go wrong.**
   - Hopper's Trustpilot score is about 1.3-1.5 out of 5 across roughly 5,700 reviews. Delayed refunds and hard-to-reach support dominate ([Trustpilot CA](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/hopper.com?page=8); [Trustpilot IE](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/hopper.com)).
   - **Use:** we are referral-only, so the partner handles bookings. Say so plainly ("you book directly with Expedia, Klook..."). That is honest, and it keeps us outside the refund mess.
3. **Inconsistent prices between sellers and price-match fine print** (itravel2000 reviewers: one price on itravel, another on Sunwing; a rep would not honour a cheaper price) ([Feefo](https://www.feefo.com/en-GB/reviews/itravel2000)). **Thin.**
4. **Flight-only tools leave the hard part to you.**
   - Google's older Destinations view showed flight + hotel estimates but did not update the total when you changed options ([Techlicious](https://techlicious.com/blog/google-destinations-a-better-way-to-plan-your-next-vacation), old article).
   - Calculators admit they are "a planning tool, not a quote".
   - **Thin as a complaint.** It is mostly inferred from how the tools work, not from angry reviews. It needs real-user evidence, which the Gate C test provides.

What I could not get: Reddit threads (search returned none, and Reddit blocks automated reading), and App Store reviews for Kayak and Skyscanner. **Next step for the owner:** spend 30 minutes reading r/PersonalFinanceCanada, r/askTO and r/travel for "where can I go for $X" posts, and paste 5-10 real phrasings into `marketing-plan.md` §6. They become ad copy.

## 5. The gap we can own

> **A budget-first, whole-trip answer for Canadians: "Here's where $X gets the two of you from Toronto, all in (flights, hotel and daily spending), in CAD, with an honest range and how sure we are."**

Evidence it is a gap:
- Every budget-first tool (Google Explore, Kayak Explore, Skyscanner Everywhere, deal newsletters) prices **airfare only** (rows 1, 3, 4, 11).
- Every whole-trip tool (packages, calculators) makes you **pick the destination first**, or sells only resort packages (rows 6-9, 12).
- The loudest industry complaint is **prices that grow at checkout** (theme 1). Ranges with confidence labels answer it directly. Nobody else shows a "low / expected / high" total.
- **Canadian specifics** are underserved by the U.S.-built tools: CAD, Toronto departures, Government of Canada travel advisories, and "know before you go" notes (drones confiscated in Colombia and Morocco, cash limits, medication rules). Travel Pirates even notes its Canadian deals are often in USD ([Travel Pirates](https://www.travelpirates.com/departures/canada)).

What the gap is **not**: cheaper prices. We send people to the same suppliers. Never claim "cheapest".

## 6. The threat

- **Google, fastest.** Explore already has a price filter, and AI Mode already assembles flights + hotels in Canvas. A "total trip under $X" toggle is a small step for them. **Cover:**
  - Be the Canadian, honest-range specialist: advisories, know-before-you-go, CAD, Toronto first.
  - Own the audience directly (email list, price-drop alerts), so a Google feature doesn't erase us.
  - Grow search content around questions Google answers badly ("$1,500 for a week from Toronto, where?").
- **Kayak.** It has Explore, a vacation-cost calculator history ([Money.com](https://money.com/new-vacation-calculator-kayak/?xid=mcclatchy)) and "Ask". It could bundle these.
- **Canadian deal newsletters** (Flytrippers, YYZ Deals). They could add a "whole trip" angle to their posts, and they already have the audience. **Cover:** they are also potential partners. Offer them our total-trip numbers for their deals.
- **Package sellers (RedTag, itravel2000).** They already sell "all-in" sun trips. They won't copy a referral tool, but they win any buyer who only wants a resort. Accept that, and position for buyers who want more than a resort.
