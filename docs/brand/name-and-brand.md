# Name and brand

Prepared 2026-11-25 with the `founder-brand` method, using the positioning in [marketing-plan.md](marketing-plan.md) and the gap in [competitors.md](competitors.md). Working title until the owner decides: **Budget Trip Finder**.

> **What these checks are and aren't.** I ran public searches only: registry lookups (RDAP/WHOIS) for domains, the CIPO and USPTO search sites for trademarks, and public profile pages for handles. Nothing has been registered, bought or created. A trademark agent or lawyer confirms clearance; this is not legal advice. Availability changes daily, so re-check on the day you register.

## 1. The brief, in one paragraph

Canadians who know what they can spend but not where to go. They want the **whole trip, all in**, in CAD, from Toronto, and an honest sense of how sure the number is. The name has to work as:
- a `.ca` and ideally a `.com`
- a 32px icon
- a TikTok handle
- a phrase people say out loud ("check it on ___")

It must not sound like a discount seller ("cheap", "deals"), because we don't sell and we don't promise the lowest price. It must not sound like a booking company ("Book", "Travel Co."), because TICO rules keep Phase 1 referral-only.

## 3. Shortlist: checks

### 3.1 Domains (registry lookups, 2026-11-25)

| Name | Exact .ca | Exact .com | Fallback .com |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Far Enough** | farenough.ca **free** | farenough.com taken (registered 2002, redirects to fareenough.com) | farenoughtrips.com **free**, farenoughtravel.com **free** (gofarenough.com taken) |
| **Getaway Math** | getawaymath.ca **free** | getawaymath.com **free** | not needed |
| **Trip Kitty** | tripkitty.ca **free** | tripkitty.com taken | thetripkitty.com **free**, gettripkitty.com **free** |

For a Canada-only product, **`.ca` as the main domain is a strength**: it signals Canadian-made and CAD prices. CIRA requires Canadian presence, which the owner has. Register the `.com` fallback too, so a stranger can't take it. Expected cost is about $15-25 a year each (PLAN.md has $15/year for one).

### 3.2 Trademarks (quick public search, not clearance)

Searched 2026-11-25. Relevant classes: **35** (advertising, comparison and referral services), **39** (travel information and arranging), **42** (software as a service, online search platforms) and **9** (downloadable app, later).
- CIPO: [Canadian Trademarks Database](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cipo/trademark-search/srch)
- USPTO: [Trademark search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search/search-information)

| Name | CIPO | USPTO | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Far Enough | "farenough": 0 results. "fare enough": 0. "far enough" (all fields): 1 unrelated hit (an abandoned e-cigarette design mark matching on description text) | No "FAR ENOUGH" or "FAREENOUGH" mark. Results are only marks containing "FAR" or "ENOUGH". Note a live "FAR" registration in class 39 (air transport), single word, different overall impression. | No obvious conflict |
| Getaway Math | "getaway math": 0 results | No "GETAWAY MATH". Many "GETAWAY" marks in unrelated classes (tires, ices, apparel) | No obvious conflict. "Getaway" alone is weak. |
| Trip Kitty | "trip kitty": 0 results | No "TRIP KITTY". Live "KITTY KITTY" (games) and many "TRIP" marks | No obvious conflict |

Before committing, the owner (or a trademark agent) should:
- search each name in classes 35, 39 and 42 with phonetic variants ("Fare Enough", "Far-Enuff")
- check Canadian business names via [NUANS](https://www.nuans.com/) or the Ontario Business Registry
- do a plain web and app-store search for travel products with the same name

Filing a Canadian application costs a few hundred dollars per class, so check CIPO's fee page. It can wait until Gate C passes; using the name first does build common-law rights in Canada.

### 3.4 Confusion check against competitors

None of the three is close to Hopper, Kayak, Skyscanner, Going, Jack's Flight Club, Triips, Flytrippers, YYZ Deals, RedTag, itravel2000 or Flight Centre. "Far Enough" shares the "fare" sound with flight-deal brands, so lead with **trips**, not fares.

### 3.5 Recommendation

1. **Far Enough** (farenough.ca + farenoughtrips.com, @farenoughtrips). It is the most memorable and the warmest. The question it implies ("how far does $2,000 go?") is the product. It works as a phrase in hooks ("Is Lisbon far enough for $2,400?").
2. **Getaway Math** (getawaymath.com + .ca, @getawaymath). The only clean exact `.com` + `.ca` pair. It is the most honest about the "we did the sums" positioning. Pick it if the owner prefers clarity over charm.
3. **Trip Kitty.** Pick it if the first audience turns out to be groups and couples pooling money. Its handles are weaker.

**Owner decides.** Suggested order: re-check on the day → register the `.ca` and fallback `.com` → claim the IG, TikTok, YouTube and X handles in one sitting → then put the name on the landing page.

## 4. Voice and promise (written for Far Enough; works for the others with the name swapped)

**One-line promise:**
> Every trip we show fits your whole budget (flights, hotel and spending money), in Canadian dollars, and we tell you how sure we are.

**Taglines, three options:**
1. **"See how far your budget goes."** (recommended: plain, says the job)
2. "The whole trip. All in. In CAD."
3. "Know the total before you fall for the place."

**Descriptor line** (under the logo and in meta titles): *Whole-trip budgets from Toronto*. This keeps "budget trip finder" for search engines without making it the name.

**Voice in three adjectives:**

| Adjective | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Honest** | Show ranges and say "estimate". Say when something is weak ("daily spending is our roughest number"). Say who you book with. | "Cheapest", "guaranteed", "best price", teaser "from $299" prices. Never "book now" or "book with us" (TICO). |
| **Plain-spoken Canadian** | CAD always. "The two of you." Toronto first. Light, dry humour ("Lisbon is far enough. Your budget agrees."). | Hockey and maple clichés, "eh", fake folksiness, Americanisms like "vacation budget hacks". |
| **Calm and practical** | Numbers first, then the nice photo. Short sentences. One clear next step. | FOMO, countdown timers, "only 2 left!", all-caps urgency, exclamation-mark piles. |

**Before/after** (current site sub-headline):
- Before: *"Tell us your total budget. We find whole trips from Toronto that fit: flights, hotel and daily spending, in CAD."*
- After: *"Got $2,000? See where it really gets you from Toronto: flights, hotel and spending money added up, in CAD, with an honest range."*

## 5. Look brief (for the site-redesign session)

### 5.1 Colour

The idea: a calm, trustworthy base (lake and night sky over Ontario) with one warm "sun" accent for the single action on each screen. It avoids the bright blue of OTAs, the red of RedTag and Air Canada, and Hopper's colour palette.

| Token | Hex | Job | Contrast (checked, WCAG 2.x formula) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--ink` | `#13293D` | Text, logo, dark mode background | 14.0:1 on paper (AAA) |
| `--lake` | `#0E5E6F` | Primary brand colour: headers, links, "expected" figure | 7.0:1 on paper, 7.4:1 white on lake (AAA) |
| `--sun` | `#F2A541` | Accent: the one call to action, the "fits your budget" badge. **Always with ink text on it**, never white. | Ink on sun 7.3:1 (AAA). Sun as text on paper is only 1.9:1, so never use it for text. |
| `--sun-deep` | `#B45309` | Accent used as small text (e.g. "over budget by $120") | 4.7:1 on paper (AA) |
| `--paper` | `#FBF8F3` | Page background (warm off-white, not stark white) | n/a |
| `--mist` | `#E6F0F2` | Cards, range bars, low/high band | Ink on mist 12.8:1 |

Range bars (the signature visual): a mist track, a lake-coloured band from low to high, and an ink tick at "expected". Confidence labels use shape plus text, not colour alone (accessibility).

### 5.2 Type (free, open licence, Google Fonts)

- **Display:** [Bricolage Grotesque](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bricolage+Grotesque) (SIL Open Font License). Friendly with character, good for big CAD numbers in headlines.
- **Text and numbers:** [Inter](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter) (SIL OFL). Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` so prices line up in columns.
- Fallback: `system-ui, sans-serif`. Self-host the two fonts or load from Google Fonts. Both are free for commercial use.

### 5.3 Logo brief

- **Must say:** "far enough", meaning distance within a limit. Idea starters:
  - a dotted flight arc that lands exactly on a horizon line
  - a range bracket `[ ]` with a small sun inside
  - a wordmark where the arc connects the "f" and the final "h"
- **Must work:**
  - 32px favicon and app icon (needs a monogram: an "fe" ligature or the arc-and-horizon mark alone)
  - one colour, for print and stickers
  - on ink (dark mode) and on paper
  - a TikTok or IG profile circle
- **Avoid:**
  - planes, globes, suitcases and map pins (every competitor uses them)
  - maple leaves (government look, and Air Canada)
  - dollar signs (reads as "cheap" or finance)
  - anything resembling Hopper's bunny or Kayak's wordmark
- If the owner has an image tool, generate 3-5 **concepts** from this brief and label them concepts. A designer (or a careful vector redraw) makes the final mark.

### 5.4 First five touchpoints

1. **Landing page (Gate C):**
   - headline "See how far your budget goes."
   - a budget box pre-filled with $2,000 for 2
   - three live example trips as cards with range bars
   - email sign-up ("Get trips that fit your budget, monthly")
   - affiliate disclosure in the footer and next to results
2. **Result card:**
   - destination, total "expected $1,940 (range $1,720-2,260)" against the budget
   - flights / hotel / spending split
   - confidence label per line
   - know-before-you-go icon
   - "See flights on Aviasales →" (partner named, not "Book")
3. **Welcome email:** "Here's what $2,000 got two people from Toronto this month" (three trips) + how our ranges work + "reply and tell us your budget".
4. **First social post:** a 20-second video, "I gave a website $1,500 and asked for a week away from Toronto" (see marketing-plan.md hooks).
5. **Reply to the first complaint ("your price was wrong"):** thank them, ask what they found and where, explain the range, say "we're using this to fix our numbers", and log it as Gate A data. Never argue, and never promise a price.
